The surface is both smooth and mute, formed out of layers where elements such as the circle pop out from the shadow behind it, or the star almost floats from the background given the halo surrounding it. Nevertheless the surface feels absorbable at a quick glance hinting at a compression or flatness of this depth. It relates to Georgia O’Keefe’s work obliquely - aligning due to the smoothness of the curves, and opposing in its tonality or range. Both are contemplative and descriptive of their environment.

Like other paintings from *resonancias, granada* captures blurred snapshots of reality due to its distorted lines, patterns and rhytmns on the canvas. Given Carabanõ’s interests in meshing and dialogue of sensations, this piece speaks of ephemerality and memory: instead of capturing the moment per se, Carabaño shows us the imprinting of a glare - as if these pieces were a form of Gestalt practice that together tries to find a whole. It is only natural that the medium itself needs to reflect this desire, suggested by the undulating frame. In this sense *granada* possibly portrays a specific generational emphasis integrating atomized pieces of existing into a meaningful core.

Furthermore, due to its scale it functions akin to a relic which is exacerbated by its setting whilst sitting on a small alcove embedded on the wall - an architectural detail typically associated with religious figures of altars or saints. In a sense it feels Carabaño is telling us this is what she chooses to believe.
