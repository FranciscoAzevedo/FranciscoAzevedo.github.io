Made with papercutting technique, the piece portrays two characters in a dance. Not only are they connected to each other via phallic symbols in the centre of the composition, but also to their surroundings, full of floral and fauna, denoting a morphing process between humans and their environment.
The vibrant palette differs substantially from traditional Chinese paper cutting, which usually features monochrome ones (red or black). Here the subversion is embodied in its plethora of colors. The artist chooses a conventional form of the papercut - commonly used to express social conformity - as a means to convey his own non-conformity.
The traditional Chinese characters symbolize deep affection and male love. Specifically, the first two refer to the story of Emperor Ai cutting his sleeve to leave his lover, who was sleeping on top of it, undisturbed.
Generally exhibited hanging on a plane of glass, its voids point to a sense of transparency - as if we were able to read through it.
